trained `learned_residual` module are a small perturbation of the pooled queries and the LLM's loss barely depends
on them, i.e. "learned queries" add nothing measurable at this data scale and the module is fixed pooling + gated
residuals + LoRA; the e002 gain over the pooled row (DocVQA +2.7, loss -0.01 nats) comes from the residual branches
moving more at compressor lr 1e-3 than at 2e-5, not from the query shift. (2) Table: the full 2x row of the
learned-query Perceiver is within the pooled row's envelope on the six remaining benchmarks.

## Interpretation
(probe part) The learned queries are inert: a 0.2% shift of the pooled query, worth 0.001 nats, and the module trained
to put nothing through its cross-attention (gate 1e-4, and the attention it would carry collapsed to near one-hot on
one native token per query, the opposite of the collapsed `learned` runs' uniform attention). Everything the trained
module does beyond fixed pooling is a per-token MLP on the pooled token (gate 0.01 x a 19-RMS branch), which moves
each token by 33% RMS at cosine 0.946 and is worth 0.031 nats under its adapter and 0.009 nats against the pooled
row's adapter+module. So at 2x on LLaVA@66K the competitive "Perceiver" rows (pooled and learned_residual) are fixed
pooling plus a small learned per-token transform; cross-attention over the native tokens is not used by either, and
whether that is a data-scale limit (66K samples, 488 steps) or a property of pooled-anchored queries cannot be told
from these two runs. The e002 gain over the pooled row (loss -0.009, DocVQA +2.7) is attributable to the higher
compressor lr letting the MLP branch move (gate 0.010 vs 0.004, weights 1.4x), not to learned queries. For the table
this means the "learned queries" row should be labelled as such in the record: it matches pooled because it is
pooled plus a per-token MLP.
